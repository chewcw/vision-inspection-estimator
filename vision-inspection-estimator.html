Coverage and optics

Choose what to solve. Distance and Focal length fit the requested object plus margin. Coverage evaluates a fixed focal length and object distance and reports any clipping.

Model: magnification m = f / (u − f); field of view = sensor size / m; finite-distance angle = 2 atan(FOV / 2u). Object distance is from the principal plane, not front-of-lens clearance. Edmund Optics: focal length and field of view.

Inspection detail

The pixel requirement is geometric. Contrast, lens MTF, demosaicing, material appearance, illumination geometry, and the inspection algorithm all affect real detection.

Model: feature pixels = smallest feature / mm per pixel; required image width = field width / (feature / pixels per feature). No arbitrary correction factor is added. Cropping or reading a smaller region of interest reduces coverage but keeps the same mm/pixel when pixel pitch and magnification are unchanged.

Motion and capture

Motion is constant speed parallel to the inspection plane. Direction is measured in the image: 0° points right and 90° points down. Global shutter does not remove exposure blur; rolling shutter adds spatial skew on top of per-row exposure.

Model: displacement = speed × light-integration time. Rolling-shutter common flash window = exposure − first-to-last-row start span. Pulse delay is measured from the first row's exposure start. Basler: image quality and motion, electronic shutter types, flash-window timing.

Lighting and exposure

Relative signal is proportional to intensity × integration time / effective f-number squared. A measured reference only scales a comparable, known usable setup; it cannot size an arbitrary lamp from electrical wattage.

Model: relative collected signal ∝ I × t / Neff2, with Neff = N × (1 + m) under a symmetric pupil assumption. Reference lux applies only with unchanged spectrum, material, optics transmission, and illumination geometry. Edmund Optics: aperture and system throughput.

Focus and aperture

Height variation is the total object-height range centered on the focused plane. The geometric focus range excludes lens aberrations and diffraction. The Airy diameter is an indicative optical scale, not a pass/fail detection threshold.

Model: symmetric thin-lens cone with defocus diameter from the smaller pixel pitch; Neff = N × (1 + m); Airy diameter = 2.44 × wavelength × Neff. Fujifilm: machine vision lens terms, Edmund Optics: depth of field, Edmund Optics: Airy disk.
